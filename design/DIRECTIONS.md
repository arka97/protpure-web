# The three directions (each Codex run builds exactly one)

## A — "Clean Room"  (slug: clean-room)
Light, precise, instrument-grade. Near-white cool surfaces, near-black ink, ONE accent (a process teal/blue, use sparingly), hairline rules, tabular monospace numerals for every spec, engineering-drawing line art (bead cross-sections, column schematics, pressure–flow curves as thin SVG line charts). Dense but airy data tables; generous margins; grid you can feel. Type: a characterful grotesk for display (e.g. Space Grotesk, Schibsted Grotesk, Instrument Sans, Bricolage Grotesque) + a mono for figures (IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono, Geist Mono). Wins with: sceptical process scientists who want Cytiva-level seriousness without the corporate blandness. Tradeoff: less warmth, relies on data density being immaculate.

## B — "Deep Field"  (slug: deep-field)
The evolution of the current identity, made distinctive. Deep navy/ink surfaces for heroes and the particle platform, luminous teal as data highlight, but the display face becomes an editorial serif (Instrument Serif, Newsreader, Source Serif 4, Spectral, Libre Caslon) — a nod to the old site's serif — paired with a clean sans for UI. A generative "bead field" motif (SVG circles of the four bead sizes, scattered/ordered) replaces the hex pattern. Real photography of columns, resin slurry and the plant, big and confident. Stats as large numerals. Wins with: Indian biopharma buyers who want an ambitious, credible domestic deep-tech brand. Tradeoff: dark sections must not swallow the data tables — keep tables on light surfaces.

## C — "Paper & Ink"  (slug: paper-and-ink)
Warm scientific-journal editorial. Off-white paper surfaces (subtly warm), ink-black text, one warm accent (oxidised copper / amber / deep emerald — pick one, with an oklch rationale), thin rules and small caps like a datasheet or monograph. Large editorial type, magazine-style asymmetric grid, scientific line illustrations (agarose bead pore structure, ligand chemistry sketches), founder-forward storytelling ("scientist to scientist"). Product pages read like a beautifully typeset technical datasheet. Type: a serif with real personality (Literata, Newsreader, Cormorant Garamond, Playfair Display — not Fraunces) + a humanist sans (Public Sans, Work Sans, Source Sans 3, Figtree). Wins with: research labs, distributors, and anyone who values authority and humanity (the Cube Biotech play, done far better). Tradeoff: must still look like a manufacturer you can order 25 L from, not a magazine.
